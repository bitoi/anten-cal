Uniform Linear Array - ULA

b

A ULA consists of N elements arranged in a straight line (usually along the Z-axis), equally spaced by a distance d.

The normalized Array Factor (AF) magnitude is calculated as:

$$ |AF| = \left| \frac{\sin\left(\frac{N\psi}{2}\right)}{N\sin\left(\frac{\psi}{2}\right)} \right| $$

Where Ψ is the total phase difference between two consecutive elements, depending on the observation angle θ and the electrical phase excitation α (used for beam steering):

$$ \psi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} d \cos(\theta) + \alpha $$

The directivity D:

$$ D = \frac{4\pi}{\int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^\pi |AF(\theta)|^2 \sin(\theta) d\theta d\phi} $$

For a ULA, the half-power beamwidth (HPBW) and first null beamwidth (FNBW) can be approximated based on the number of elements N and the element spacing d:

$$ HPBW \approx \frac{102}{N \cdot d/\lambda} \text{ (degrees)} $$ $$ FNBW \approx \frac{180}{N \cdot d/\lambda} \text{ (degrees)} $$

Uniform Planar Array (UPA)

UPA is the combination of two uniform linear arrays placed perpendicular to each other on the X-Y plane (forming an \(N_x\) x \(N_y\) matrix). The total Array Factor is the product of the two component AFs along the X and Y axes:

$$ |AF_{UPA}| = |AF_x| \times |AF_y| $$

Since the array is located on the X-Y plane, the natural maximum radiation direction (Broadside) will be directed vertically along the Z-axis. The phase difference component along each axis depends on both the tilt angle θ and the azimuth angle ϕ:

$$ \psi_x = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} d_x \sin(\theta)\cos(\phi) + \beta_x $$ $$ \psi_y = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} d_y \sin(\theta)\sin(\phi) + \beta_y $$
